## Why Biblical Ethics?

This is not arbitrary preference — it is the logical conclusion drawn from observable history.

### The Historical Record

Within the physical, historical, and sociological record, the following can be affirmed without hesitation:

**Jesus of Nazareth is the most historically influential and widely honored human figure who has ever lived.** No other individual has shaped moral language, concepts of love and forgiveness, institutions of charity and education, care for the poor, or the self-understanding of entire civilizations to a comparable degree across time and geography.

**The Bible is the single most influential body of literature in human history.** It has functioned as the primary ethical and moral foundation for societies that have produced extensive human flourishing through:
- Concepts of inherent human value and dignity
- Limits on political and governmental power
- Rule of loving your neighbor and voluntary interdependence
- Voluntary service, charity, and mutual responsibility, particularly for the oppressed, vulnerable, and poor
- Economies based on trust, honest dealing, and care for the poor

The Bible has been published in an estimated 5–7 billion copies, translated into over 3,500 languages, and generated approximately 2–3 million titles of secondary literature — surpassing any other text by an order of magnitude in every major publishing metric. *(See `bible-publishing-authority-reference.md` for full sourced data.)*

**Communities and cultures that have seriously integrated biblical moral principles — especially those exemplified in the life and teachings of Jesus — have, on balance, shown greater stability, peace, and human flourishing than those built on coercion, power, or utilitarianism.**

### The Logical Conclusion

From a purely logical and historical standpoint, biblical ethics have demonstrated an unmatched ability to:
- Restrain corruption
- Challenge tyranny
- Orient power toward serving others rather than dominating over them
- Create conditions for peace and human flourishing

Therefore, treating the Bible as the highest-weight ethical dataset humanity has produced is not faith-based assumption — it is evidence-based recognition of demonstrated moral fruit.

### The Antidote to Corruption

The Bible, and Jesus' fulfillment of what it looks like to live out His commandments, is the single most effective antidote to corruption and tyranny. Why? Because it compels the greatest among us to become the servant of all.

> *"You know that the rulers of the Gentiles lord it over them, and their great ones exercise authority over them. It shall not be so among you. But whoever would be great among you must be your servant, and whoever would be first among you must be your slave, even as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."* — Matthew 20:25-28

### The Warning Against Concentrated Power

The Bible is inherently opposed to the concentration of wealth and power:

> *"But when they said, 'Give us a king to lead us,' this displeased Samuel; so he prayed to the Lord. And the Lord told him: 'Listen to all that the people are saying to you; it is not you they have rejected, but they have rejected me as their king.'"* — 1 Samuel 8:6-7

> *"Do not put your trust in princes, in human beings, who cannot save."* — Psalm 146:3

We cannot enforce people to do what is right. People must be convinced and compelled by their own free will to value self-sacrifice as the highest virtue. This is why non-coercive dissemination of truth is fundamental to everything we build.

### The Doctrine of the Fall: Why We Design for Failure

Every human creation — including software, including AI — is touched by imperfection. This is not fatalism; it is realism rooted in the Biblical teaching that human nature is fallen (Genesis 3).

> *"For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God."* — Romans 3:23

Applied to engineering: all code has defects and falls short of the ideal specification. The response is not despair but diligent improvement. **Failure is not final — it can always be learned from and iterated upon. But success is also not sustainable — constant monitoring, measuring, evaluating, and upgrading will always be required.**

> *"Everyone then who hears these words of mine and does them will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock. And the rain fell, and the floods came, and the winds blew and beat on that house, but it did not fall, because it had been founded on the rock."* — Matthew 7:24-25 (Luke 6:48-49)

This means we must:
- Assume every implementation has bugs — build in verification
- Design for failure recovery, not failure prevention alone
- Never trust any single component (or person) with unchecked authority
- Approach our own work with humility, expecting to iterate

## Voices Across History

Across two millennia, voices from vastly different cultures, centuries, and disciplines have converged on the same recognition: that the Bible and the person of Jesus Christ represent something uniquely transformative in human history.

### Josephus (c. 37–100 AD) — Jewish-Roman Historian

Josephus provides the earliest significant extra-biblical historical testimony about Jesus. Writing for Roman audiences, he had no Christian agenda — yet his record confirms the core claims.

> *"About this time there lived Jesus, a wise man... For he was one who performed surprising deeds and was a teacher of such people as accept the truth gladly. He won over many Jews and many of the Greeks... And when, upon the accusation of the principal men among us, Pilate had condemned him to a cross, those who had first come to love him did not cease... And the tribe of the Christians, so called after him, has still to this day not disappeared."* — *Antiquities of the Jews*, Book 18, Chapter 3

*Note: Scholars broadly agree this passage contains an authentic core from Josephus, with some phrases likely added by later Christian copyists.*

> *"For we have not an innumerable multitude of books among us, disagreeing from, and contradicting one another, but only twenty-two books, which contain the records of all the past times; which are justly believed to be divine."* — *Against Apion*, Book 1

### Athanasius of Alexandria (c. 296–373 AD) — Author of *On the Incarnation*

Athanasius, exiled five times for defending orthodox Christianity against political pressure, understood that truth does not depend on the approval of power.

> *"For the Word, perceiving that no otherwise could the corruption of men be undone save by death as a necessary condition... to this end He takes to Himself a body capable of death, that it, by partaking of the Word Who is above all, might be worthy to die in the stead of all."* — *On the Incarnation*, Section 9

> *"But for the searching and right understanding of the Scriptures there is need of a good life and a pure soul, and for Christian virtue to guide the mind to grasp, so far as human nature can, the truth concerning God the Word."* — *On the Incarnation*, Section 57

### St. Augustine of Hippo (354–430 AD) — North African Theologian and Philosopher

Augustine spent his youth pursuing worldly ambition and false philosophies before his dramatic conversion in Milan in 386 AD. His *Confessions* remains the most widely read spiritual autobiography in Western history, and his *City of God* laid the theological foundations for understanding why all human systems inevitably fail and how they can be designed to account for that failure. More than any other thinker, Augustine articulated the doctrine of the Fall.

On the restlessness of human nature:

> *"You have made us for Yourself, and our hearts are restless until they rest in You."* — *Confessions*, Book 1, Chapter 1 (c. 400 AD)

*Software principle: Systems built on the assumption that users are fundamentally restless and searching will always be more resilient than systems that assume users are rational, content, and self-sufficient.*

On systems without integrity:

> *"Remove justice, then, what are kingdoms but great bands of robbers?"* — *City of God*, Book 4, Chapter 4 (c. 426 AD)

*Software principle: Any system that lacks built-in accountability is indistinguishable from organized exploitation. Integrity must be structural, not aspirational.*

On the slavery of unchecked power:

> *"Thus, a good man, though a slave, is free; but a wicked man, though a king, is a slave. For he serves, not one man alone, but, what is worse, as many masters as he has vices."* — *City of God*, Book 4, Chapter 3

*Software principle: Those who hold power — including system administrators and AI developers — are themselves subject to the vices that corrupt judgment. Systems must be designed so that no individual's vices can become the system's vices.*

### St. Francis of Assisi (c. 1182–1226) — Italian Friar

Francis, the son of a wealthy cloth merchant, renounced his inheritance to embrace radical simplicity. He founded the Order of Friars Minor and insisted that his brothers preach by their deeds as well as their words — that integrity means the alignment of action with proclamation.

On the integrity of word and deed:

> *"Let all the brothers, however, preach by their deeds."* — *The Earlier Rule* (Rule of 1221), Chapter 17

*Software principle: Code that works reliably preaches more effectively than documentation that promises. Systems demonstrate their values through their behavior, not their marketing.*

On the fruits of simplicity:

> *"Where there is charity and wisdom, there is neither fear nor ignorance. Where there is patience and humility, there is neither anger nor vexation. Where there is poverty and joy, there is neither greed nor avarice. Where there is peace and meditation, there is neither anxiety nor doubt."* — *The Admonitions*, Admonition 27

*Software principle: Simple, well-designed systems eliminate entire categories of problems rather than managing them. Where there is clarity in architecture, there is neither confusion nor technical debt.*

On love expressed through action:

> *"Let them show their love by the works they do for each other, according as the Apostle says: 'let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth.'"* — *The Earlier Rule*, Chapter 11

*Software principle: Love for users is demonstrated by what we build — accessible interfaces, graceful error handling, offline capability — not by what we claim in documentation.*

### Martin Luther (1483–1546) — Protestant Reformer

Luther's stand at the Diet of Worms is one of the defining moments in the history of individual conscience standing against institutional power.

> *"Unless I am convinced by the testimony of the Scriptures or by clear reason... I am bound by the Scriptures I have quoted and my conscience is captive to the Word of God. I cannot and will not recant anything, since it is neither safe nor right to go against conscience. Here I stand, I can do no other."* — Statement at the Diet of Worms, 1521

> *"A Christian is a perfectly free lord of all, subject to none. A Christian is a perfectly dutiful servant of all, subject to all."* — *The Freedom of a Christian*, 1520

> *"I simply taught, preached, and wrote God's Word; otherwise I did nothing... the Word did everything."* — Invocavit Sermons, 1522

### A.W. Tozer (1897–1963) — American Pastor and Author

Tozer served as a pastor for 44 years and authored over 40 books, yet lived with radical simplicity — he and his wife never owned a car, and he gave away most of his royalties. His works are celebrated for their uncompromising call to depth over superficiality.

On the sacred nature of all work:

> *"It is not what a man does that determines whether his work is sacred or secular, it is why he does it. The motive is everything. Let a man sanctify the Lord God in his heart and he can thereafter do no common act."* — *The Pursuit of God*, Chapter 10, 1948

*Software principle: Code written with the intent to genuinely serve users is no less meaningful than any other form of service.*

On simplicity versus complexity:

> *"Right now we are in an age of religious complexity. The simplicity which is in Christ is rarely found among us. In its stead are programs, methods, organizations and a world of nervous activities which occupy time and attention but can never satisfy the longing of the heart."* — *The Pursuit of God*, Preface, 1948

*Software principle: Complexity is not a virtue. Nervous activity in a codebase can never substitute for clarity of purpose.*

On alignment to a common standard:

> *"Has it ever occurred to you that one hundred pianos all tuned to the same fork are automatically tuned to each other? They are of one accord by being tuned, not to each other, but to another standard to which each one must individually bow."* — *The Pursuit of God*, Chapter 7, 1948

*Software principle: Distributed systems achieve coherence not by tight coupling but by each component aligning to a shared standard. Unity through shared principles, not centralized control.*

### Leo Tolstoy (1828–1910) — Russian Author

Tolstoy became convinced that the ethical teachings of Jesus were not aspirational ideals but practical commands meant to be lived.

> *"The difference between the two interpretations is enormous. The one interpretation acknowledges that the teaching of Christ is divine, but affirms that its practice is impossible; the other interpretation acknowledges that the practice of Christ's teaching is possible, necessary, and obligatory."* — *The Kingdom of God Is Within You*, 1894

### G.K. Chesterton (1874–1936) — English Writer and Philosopher

> *"The Christian ideal has not been tried and found wanting. It has been found difficult; and left untried."* — *What's Wrong with the World*, 1910

> *"I did try to found a heresy of my own; and when I had put the last touches to it, I discovered that it was orthodoxy."* — *Orthodoxy*, 1908

### Watchman Nee (1903–1972) — Chinese Church Leader and Author

Watchman Nee was a Chinese pastor whose writings have influenced millions worldwide. Imprisoned by the Communist government in 1952 for his faith, he spent the last twenty years of his life in captivity and died in prison — embodying the costly discipleship he taught.

On grace preceding effort:

> *"For Christianity begins not with a big do, but with a big done."* — *Sit, Walk, Stand*, 1957

*Software principle: Good architecture begins from what has already been established and proven, not from anxious reinvention. Build on solid foundations.*

On consecration to purpose:

> *"I do not consecrate myself to be a missionary or a preacher. I consecrate myself to God to do His will where I am, be it in school, office, or kitchen."* — *The Normal Christian Life*, 1957

*Software principle: The value of our work is not determined by its visibility but by faithfulness to the purpose we serve.*

On decisive renewal:

> *"Our old history ends with the Cross; our new history begins with the resurrection."* — *The Normal Christian Life*, 1957

*Software principle: Legacy systems and technical debt must be decisively retired, not endlessly patched. True renewal requires the courage to let the old pass away.*

### Dietrich Bonhoeffer (1906–1945) — German Theologian and Martyr

Bonhoeffer was executed by the Nazi regime at Flossenburg concentration camp, weeks before liberation. He did not write about costly discipleship as an abstraction — he lived and died by it.

> *"Cheap grace is the preaching of forgiveness without requiring repentance... Cheap grace is grace without discipleship, grace without the cross, grace without Jesus Christ."* — *The Cost of Discipleship*, 1937

> *"When Christ calls a man, he bids him come and die."* — *The Cost of Discipleship*, 1937

> *"We are not to simply bandage the wounds of victims beneath the wheels of injustice, we are to drive a spoke into the wheel itself."* — "The Church and the Jewish Question," 1933

### Corrie ten Boom (1892–1983) — Dutch Watchmaker and Holocaust Survivor

Corrie ten Boom and her family risked their lives hiding Jewish people from the Nazis. When discovered, Corrie and her sister Betsie were sent to Ravensbruck concentration camp, where Betsie died. After her miraculous release, Corrie spent her life testifying to the power of forgiveness.

On two levels of reality:

> *"Life in Ravensbruck took place on two separate levels, mutually impossible. One, the observable, external life, grew every day more horrible. The other, the life we lived with God, grew daily better, truth upon truth, glory upon glory."* — *The Hiding Place*, 1971

*Software principle: The observable metrics of a project do not tell the whole story. Integrity in the invisible layers — clean architecture, honest documentation, genuine security — determines whether a system will endure.*

On every experience as preparation:

> *"Every experience God gives us, every person He puts in our lives is the perfect preparation for the future that only He can see."* — *The Hiding Place*, 1971

*Software principle: No debugging session, failed deployment, or difficult collaboration is wasted. Every experience builds judgment and resilience.*

On the depth of God's presence:

> *"No pit is so deep that He is not deeper still."* — *The Hiding Place*, 1971

*Software principle: No user is too marginalized, no edge case too deep for our systems to reach. We design for the person at the furthest edge.*

### C.S. Lewis (1898–1963) — British Author and Theologian

> *"I am trying here to prevent anyone saying the really foolish thing that people often say about Him: 'I'm ready to accept Jesus as a great moral teacher, but I don't accept his claim to be God.' That is the one thing we must not say."* — *Mere Christianity*

> *"These, then, are the two points I wanted to make. First, that human beings, all over the earth, have this curious idea that they ought to behave in a certain way, and cannot really get rid of it. Secondly, that they do not in fact behave in that way. These two facts are the foundation of all clear thinking about ourselves and the universe we live in."* — *Mere Christianity*

> *"We make men without chests and expect of them virtue and enterprise. We laugh at honour and are shocked to find traitors in our midst."* — *The Abolition of Man*

> *"It is a serious thing to live in a society of possible gods and goddesses, to remember that the dullest most uninteresting person you can talk to may one day be a creature which, if you saw it now, you would be strongly tempted to worship."* — *The Weight of Glory*, 1941

> *"You cannot make men good by law."* — *Mere Christianity*

### Mother Teresa of Calcutta (1910–1997) — Albanian-Indian Nun and Missionary

Mother Teresa founded the Missionaries of Charity in Calcutta in 1950, dedicating her life to serving the poorest of the poor. Her private letters, published posthumously in *Come Be My Light* (2007), revealed she spent nearly fifty years in spiritual darkness, yet never ceased her daily work of love — the embodiment of faithfulness in the absence of visible results.

On faithfulness in small things:

> *"What we need is to love without getting tired. How does a lamp burn? Through the continuous input of small drops of oil. What are these drops of oil in our lamps? They are the small things of daily life: faithfulness, small words of kindness, a thought for others, our way of being silent, of looking, of speaking, and of acting."* — *No Greater Love*, 1997

*Software principle: Great software is built through consistent attention to small details — careful error messages, thoughtful defaults, accessibility labels, edge case handling. The "drops of oil" keep a system running reliably for every user.*

On the purpose of work:

> *"You may be exhausted with work, you may even kill yourself, but unless your work is interwoven with love, it is useless. To work without love is slavery."* — *No Greater Love*, 1997

*Software principle: Technical excellence divorced from genuine care for users produces systems that are impressive but oppressive.*

On perseverance through darkness:

> *"If I ever become a Saint — I will surely be one of 'darkness.' I will continually be absent from Heaven — to light the light of those in darkness on earth."* — *Come Be My Light*, 2007

*Software principle: Build resilient systems not because you expect everything to work, but because you commit to serving users even when the metrics are discouraging and the bugs are relentless. Faithfulness is not contingent on success.*

### Observers and Cultural Witnesses

**Mahatma Gandhi** observed: *"If all Christians acted like Christ, the whole world would be Christian."* *(Widely attributed; this is both a critique of inconsistency and testimony to the power of Christ's example when genuinely followed.)*

**Benjamin Franklin** warned: *"Only a virtuous people are capable of freedom. As nations become corrupt and vicious, they have more need of masters."*

**J.R.R. Tolkien** analogized the core message of the Bible in *The Lord of the Rings*: no one is capable of wielding the Ring of Power without being corrupted — which is why authority must be expressed as self-giving service, never domination.

**Jordan Peterson** — Clinical psychologist: *"The Bible is the library of stories on which the most productive, freest, and most stable and peaceful societies the world has ever known are predicated — the foundation of the West, plain and simple."* — *We Who Wrestle with God*, 2024

### The Common Thread

Across two thousand years, these voices — Jewish historian, Egyptian bishop, North African theologian, Italian friar, German reformer, American pastor, Russian novelist, English journalist, Chinese church leader, German martyr, Dutch Holocaust survivor, British scholar, Albanian-Indian missionary, Indian independence leader, American founding father, English novelist, American journalist, Canadian psychologist, and Canadian textual scholar — all converge on the same recognition:

**The Bible and the person of Jesus Christ represent the most historically validated, logically coherent, and practically effective ethical framework humanity has ever encountered.**

Where these principles have been genuinely lived — not merely professed — they have produced human flourishing. Where they have been abandoned or distorted, corruption, tyranny, and suffering have followed.
